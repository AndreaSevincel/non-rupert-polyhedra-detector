Level 1: Explanatory Articles & History

    "First shape found that can't pass through itself" by Erica Klarreich (Quanta Magazine, October 2025). Explains the history of Prince Rupert's wager and the recent discovery of the "Noperthedron".

    "Prince Rupert's Rectangles" by R. P. Jerrard and J. E. Wetzel (The American Mathematical Monthly, 2004). Details the history from Prince Rupert to Pieter Nieuwland and explains the geometry of finding the optimal constant using 2D projections.

    John Carlos Baez's Blog/Thread on Rupert's Property (August 2025). A summary explaining the importance of the recent discoveries in this field.

Level 2: Foundational Mathematical Papers

    "Platonic Passages" by R. P. Jerrard, J. E. Wetzel, and L. Yuan (Mathematics Magazine, 2017). Proved that the dodecahedron and icosahedron are Rupert, and famously conjectured that every convex polyhedron in 3D space has the property.

    "Rupert Property of Archimedean Solids" by Y. Chai, L. Yuan, and T. Zamfirescu (The American Mathematical Monthly, 2018). Proves that at least ten of the thirteen Archimedean solids possess the Rupert property.

    "The Truncated Tetrahedron Is Rupert" by Gérard Lavau (The American Mathematical Monthly, 2019). Demonstrates that the truncated tetrahedron possesses the property.

Level 3: Algorithmic & "Local" Approaches (Relevant to Project Step 3)

    "Two sufficient conditions for a polyhedron to be (locally) Rupert" by Evan Scott (arXiv, 2022). Simplifies the problem by analyzing how small arbitrary rotations act on polyhedra to open a geometric passage.

    "An algorithmic approach to Rupert's problem" by Jakob Steininger and Sergey Yurkevich (Mathematics of Computation, 2023). Proves that deciding the property is algorithmically solvable for shapes with algebraic coordinates, utilizing a probabilistic algorithm.

Level 4: The Pinnacle of Current Research

    "A convex polyhedron without Rupert's property" by Jakob Steininger and Sergey Yurkevich (arXiv, August 2025). Disproves the 2017 conjecture by constructing the "Noperthedron" (a shape with 90 vertices and 152 faces) and proving it cannot pass through itself.

    "Some New Insights from Highly Optimized Polyhedral Passages" by Raj Gosain and Benjamin Grimmer (arXiv, September 2025). Proposes a customized subgradient optimization algorithm to find the exact maximum Nieuwland constant for polyhedra.
